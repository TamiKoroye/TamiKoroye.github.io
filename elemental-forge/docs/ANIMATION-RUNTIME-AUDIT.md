# Runtime animation audit (Stage 1 acceptance)

## Which build was where (15 Nov 2026)
- **Public link** (tamikoroye.github.io/elemental-forge): the earlier procedural build. `js/hero.js`, `js/mocap.js` and `assets/animations/martial.json` all return 404 there, so a recording made on that link shows no authored motion at all.
- **Private preview** (claude.ai artifact): before this audit, every file matched development commit `7dda8a7` byte for byte, including the 1,951,159-byte animation pack. It now matches commit `78bbbfd` byte for byte, including the 1,999,084-byte pack.
- **Build stamp:** the menu shows `dev 2026-11-15 r3 · commit 78bbbfd · 68 martial-arts clips loaded`. If it shows anything else, or "NO martial-arts clips", the device is not running this build. `?debug=anim` shows the live clip, its source take, speed, leg layer and the authored share of the body.

## What ordinary touch play exposed (before the fixes, commit 7dda8a7)
These were measured with the same move script, played through the touch controls and through the demo method:
- **Moving under upper-body actions.** Running, strafing or backing up under a punch, the guard, a held technique or the combat stance switched the legs to the old procedural walk. The authored share of the lower body averaged 65% in touch play and fell to 5–20% while moving, against 92% in the demo method, which stood still. This was the largest visible difference.
- **Taps turned into holds.** A HAND press longer than 0.22 s became the sustained technique and abandoned the punch. Firm thumb taps of 230–270 ms therefore produced one-frame holds instead of punches.
- **Slow-motion punch.** The alternate Earth punch (CMU lunge punch) played at 0.6× speed, because both alternating punches shared one timing.
- **Procedural charge-up.** The charge-up before each charged technique was the old procedural pose (7% authored).
- **Procedural forward and backward dodges.** These fell back to procedural motion (12–16% authored); only side dodges had a clip.
- **Combos cut short.** Strikes were cut at about 60% of the clip and the next strike faded in over 0.1 s, which read as snapping.

## Fixes (commit 78bbbfd)
- **Authored footwork under upper-body actions.** When moving under a punch, guard, hold or the stance, the clip's upper-body tracks play over authored footwork:
  - walk, jog or sprint going forward
  - the reversed walk going backward
  - a sidestep baked from the supplied "Standing Aim Walk Left" (mirrored for right) going sideways

  Legs are no longer procedural in any of these states.
- **Hold thresholds:** a hold starts at 0.30 s on HAND and 0.38 s on LEG.
- **Charged trigger:** the charged technique is judged on the total hold (at least 0.85 s), so a kick's hit-stop can't swallow it.
- **Alternating takes** each have their own timing. No clip now plays below normal speed.
- **Charge-up** holds the charged technique's own authored wind-up.
- **Dodges:** straight dodges use the authored roll.
- **Combo timing:** the next strike may start 0.22 s after the impact, so the follow-through shows, and chained strikes crossfade over 0.14 s.

## Results from the DEPLOYED development build (r5, commit 042214e)
Recorded from https://tamikoroye.github.io/elemental-forge-dev/ through the touch controls, with the rival on Normal and energy never refilled. The tables below come from that session; the earlier comparison against the demo method is in the git history of this file.

Generated by `tests/gameplay-audit.mjs` and `tests/audit-report.mjs`. The game was played in Chromium at a phone landscape viewport.

**Two sessions, same move script.** Each covers all four elements: run in and attack while running, a combination, a side dodge and counter, guard, special, strafe and attack, a held HAND and a charged LEG.
- **touch:** only the on-screen controls (CDP multi-touch on the real buttons and joystick), with taps of 70-270 ms, the joystick held while attacking, element switches through the element buttons, and the rival AI fighting back.
- **demo:** the method used for the earlier demonstration videos. Presses are written straight into the controls, the rival is passive and the player does not move.

**Columns:**
- **clip:** the clip the character actually showed (from the per-frame log)
- **source:** the original file and the segment used
- **speed:** playback rate against the capture
- **shown:** the median share of the clip that played before the next action took over
- **authored:** the share of the upper and lower body driven by authored clips rather than procedural poses
- **ended by:** what ended each instance
